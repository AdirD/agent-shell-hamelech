Bizy · Problem Discovery

Should you build Bizy?

Short answer: the problem is real and people already pay to fix it — but Bizy would be entering a crowded, funded race, and its core rule ("we don't touch the site") points the wrong way.

The problem: green light Bizy as designed: needs a rethink

The 30-second version

  • The pain is real. AI-built sites break in production — the same handful of ways, every time.
  • People already pay. $150–$15k to fix a broken AI site; ongoing retainers from $500/mo.
  • The money follows the fix, not the report. "Just tell me what's wrong" is a $10–30/mo commodity.
  • You're not early to an empty space. YC-backed startups are shipping this exact loop right now.
  • Best buyer: the agency. They'll happily resell it under their own brand across many client sites.

Question 1

Is the pain real? Yes — strongly.

This isn't a few loud people online. There's a whole industry that only exists because AI-built sites keep breaking after launch. Independent write-ups describe the same failure modes over and over: blank dashboards after a traffic spike, leaked payment keys, checkout that works once then dies, SEO that vanishes because AI crawlers see an empty page.

64%
of AI-generated payment integrations fail a basic checkout check (Stripe's own benchmark). One builder: "12 apps, the same 6 things broke every time."

Question 2

Will people pay? Yes — but for the cure, not the diagnosis.

Every source points the same way: telling someone what's wrong is cheap; making it right is where the money is. Sentry (the big monitoring company) deliberately isn't "we show you problems" — it won by moving people to "here's how to fix it."

Just knowing — dashboards, audits, reports
$5–30/mo
Diagnosing — prioritized "here's what's broken"
$150–800
Fixed & proven — rescue shops, Sentry+AI fixes
$2.5k–200k

Bizy's defining rule — "we observe and explain but don't change the site" — plants it on the cheap top rung. That's the core tension.

Question 3

Who actually buys it? The agency — and they'll resell it.

This is the good news. "Vibe-coding agencies" are now a real, named segment (Octave, Whitelabel AI, Groovy Web) selling AI-built sites with maintenance retainers from $499/mo. And they're not precious about manual work — they're racing to automate delivery and jump from ~20% margins to 70%+.

3–10× markup
what agencies already charge reselling white-label monitoring dashboards under their own brand. The reseller motion is mature — 1,200+ agencies on a single platform.

Translation: agencies are a channel, not a wall. Bizy as "the ops layer an agency runs across all its client sites, branded as theirs" is a much stronger story than "solo founder's control room."

Question 4

Who else is doing this? A lot of funded people — right now.

This is the part I have to be blunt about. Bizy's "observe → prioritize → fix → verify" loop is not an empty category. In 2026 it's an active land grab, and several rivals already close the loop and do the fix Bizy refuses to.

The uncomfortable knot

The money is on the fix. The competitors do the fix. The "just observe and explain" lane is a $10–30/mo commodity that website builders (Wix, Replit) are already bundling in for free. Bizy's one firm rule — don't change the site — sits it squarely in the cheapest, most contested lane, against funded players who go further.

The bottom line

Build it — but not the version in the deck.

The problem is validated; don't second-guess that. What needs to change is the shape. Two moves the evidence is basically shouting:

1
Sell through agencies, across many sites
Not the solo founder who ships and forgets. The agency with a book of client sites, a retainer, and an appetite to resell your tool as their own. Recurring, multi-site, budget already there.
2
Get closer to the proven fix, or you're a $12 dashboard
Make "the fix actually landed — proven from the outside, on any stack, with zero setup" the hero. That's the one square the funded rivals (code-embedded, single-site, auto-fixers) haven't nailed — and it's what people actually pay for.
The one test that settles it

Offer the same 8 agencies two things and see which one they put a card down for:

A — "A prioritized report of what's wrong on your client sites, every deploy."
B — "We brief your coding agent and then prove the fix worked — resolved, regressed, or inconclusive."

If B wins and they'll pay a premium → build around proving the fix. If A ≈ B → you're in a price fight, and you should get into the fix itself.
