# Market Validation Artifacts

Use one living brief while validation is active and one final dossier when a
decision is ready. Do not create a document per phase.

## 1. Living validation brief

Create this when validation will span research, customer access, experiments, or
multiple sessions. Use a user-chosen location or a clear workspace filename such
as `<hypothesis>-market-validation.md`.

Keep it concise and update it in place:

```markdown
# Market validation: <short hypothesis>

## Validation contract
Customer:
Situation:
Problem:
Current alternative:
Buyer:
Proposed value:
Market boundary:
Decision and deadline:

## Decision thresholds
Advance:
Reshape:
Hold:
Stop:

## Claim map
| Claim | State | Supporting evidence | Counter-evidence | Open gap | Next test |

## Evidence log
| Date | Claim | Evidence | Type/tier | Source | Supports/contradicts | Limits |

## Fieldwork and experiments
| Test | Precommitted threshold | Result | Interpretation | Decision impact |

## Current call
Status:
Why:
Next gate:

## Hypothesis changes
| Date | Changed from/to | Why | Evidence still applicable |
```

Keep facts, participant statements, and interpretation visibly separate. Link
source material instead of pasting entire transcripts.

## 2. Interim validation brief

Use when desk research is complete but customer or behavioral evidence is still
missing. It must say **Interim — research-supported, not market-validated** near
the title.

Structure:

1. hypothesis and decision
2. provisional call: field test / reshape / hold / stop
3. claim-by-claim evidence state
4. strongest supporting and contradicting evidence
5. customer, buyer, alternatives, and reachability
6. evidence coverage and limitations
7. one run-ready primary or behavioral test
8. exact threshold and what it can establish

Do not use green “validated” badges in an interim artifact.

## 3. Final market-validation dossier

Default to a standalone styled HTML page when the user wants a durable decision
artifact; use Markdown when requested or when the result belongs in an existing
documentation workflow.

### Opening

- title and validation contract
- **Advance / Reshape / Hold / Stop**
- one-sentence reason
- explicit statement of what has and has not been validated

### Validation scorecard

Show each separately:

| Area | Evidence state | Confidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer and situation | unsupported / plausible / behavior-supported / commitment-supported | high / medium / low | supported / mixed / contradicted |
| Problem | ... | ... | ... |
| Existing demand | ... | ... | ... |
| Buyer and budget | ... | ... | ... |
| Offer fit | ... | ... | ... |
| Reachability | ... | ... | ... |

Do not collapse this into one numeric score.

### Evidence progression

Tell the story in the order confidence was earned:

1. **What existing and secondary evidence established**
2. **What customer discovery changed**
3. **What behavior or commercial commitment showed**
4. **What counter-evidence survived**

Every consequential external claim needs a source or an explicit provenance
label such as first-party aggregate, interview, observed workflow, or experiment.

### Market reality

Include only what affects the decision:

- recurring customer situation and consequence
- buyer, budget path, and approval friction
- current alternatives, spend, and reasons customers stay
- competitors and structural market constraints
- reachable first segment and channel evidence
- offer, price, and switching implications

### Experiment result

Show:

- precommitted claim and threshold
- participant and offer
- observed action
- result against threshold
- validity threats and limits

Do not rewrite the threshold after seeing the result.

### Decision

For **Advance**, state the next reversible investment and remaining risk.

For **Reshape**, name exactly what changes: customer, situation, problem, buyer,
offer, price, channel, or timing. Preserve evidence that still applies.

For **Hold**, name the missing evidence and why collecting it is not currently
worth the cost.

For **Stop**, identify the contradicted claim and avoid inventing a pivot merely
to keep momentum.

### Coverage appendix

Include:

- research dates and search cap
- independent sources after deduplication
- evidence by tier and claim
- participant criteria, count, and recruiting routes
- experiment sample and duration
- known biases and unresolved contradictions
- linked source list

## Voice and visual hierarchy

- Lead with the decision, not methodology.
- Use direct human language without manufacturing certainty.
- Make negative and null results as visible as supporting evidence.
- Put confidence beside the claim it qualifies.
- Prefer sourced numbers and observed actions over adjectives.
- Use progressive disclosure: decision first, evidence next, methodology last.
- Never decorate weak evidence into looking strong.
